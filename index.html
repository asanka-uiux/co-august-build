The build story

Can you really
do it in a weekend?

Custom Offsets Build Team . August 2026 . TK Min Read

Most build videos start in a shop. Lift in the floor, air lines in the ceiling, a wall of tools somebody else paid for. That is not where your truck lives. Your truck lives in a driveway, next to a garage that is already half full, and the only window you get is Saturday and Sunday.

So we gave ourselves the same window. A stock 2018 F-150, a pile of boxes, and until Monday morning. No welding, no cutting, no powder coat, and nothing you would not find in a normal household garage. If it needed a shop, it did not go on the truck.

Start with the suspension, because everything else waits on it

The Rough Country Vertex 2.5 RR coilovers went on first for one reason: every wheel and tire decision after them depends on ride height. Two inches up front changes what fits, what rubs, and what your fender gap looks like in a photo. Doing it in any other order means measuring twice and guessing once.

TK_FAQ_1 Answer honestly, including what took longer than expected and which single job a beginner should hand off.

Offset is the distance between the wheel's mounting surface and the middle of the wheel, measured in millimeters. A high positive number pulls the wheel inward, toward the suspension. A negative number pushes it outward, toward the fender. On this build the 20x9 sits at +1mm, the 22x9.5 at +44mm, and the 22x12 at -51mm, which is why the last one looks so much wider even though the truck never changed.

Backspacing measures the same thing in inches from the back face of the wheel, and most people find it easier to picture: roughly 5in on the 20x9, 7in on the 22x9.5, and 4.5in on the 22x12.

Yes, every time. Lifting the truck changes camber and toe, and a new offset changes the scrub radius on top of it. Drive gently to the shop and get it aligned before you put real miles on a new set of tires. Budget it into your Monday, not your weekend.

No. The exact part numbers here are for a 2018 F-150 5.0L, but the recipe travels. Suspension, wheels and tires, lighting, intake, exhaust, and a tuner is the same list on almost any half ton. Run your own year, make, model, and trim through the fitment tool and you will get the same groups with different part numbers.

Suspension first, because ride height decides your wheel and tire fitment. Lighting second, since it is fast and gives you a visible win early. Performance third.
