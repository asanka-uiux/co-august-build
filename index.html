The build story

Can you really do it in a weekend?

Custom Offsets Build Team . August 2026 . 3 Min Read

Most build videos start in a shop. Lift in the floor, air lines in the ceiling, a wall of tools somebody else paid for. That is not where your truck lives. Your truck lives in a driveway, next to a garage that is already half full, and the only window you get is Saturday and Sunday.

So we gave ourselves the same window. A stock 2018 F-150, a pile of boxes, and until Monday morning. No welding, no cutting, no powder coat, and nothing you would not find in a normal household garage. If it needed a shop, it did not go on the truck.

Start with the suspension, because everything else waits on it

The Rough Country Vertex 2.5 RR coilovers went on first for one reason: every wheel and tire decision after them depends on ride height. Three inches up front changes what fits, what rubs, and what your fender gap looks like in a photo. Doing it in any other order means measuring twice and guessing once.

Lighting, because it is the fastest win you will get all weekend

Morimoto XB heads and tails are plug and play, which on a weekend build is worth more than any spec on the box. You get a visual change in under an hour that makes the truck read as newer than it is.

Performance, where the truck finally starts to sound like a build

The AWE 0FG exhaust, the aFe Momentum XP intake, and the Flashcal all went on as a group. None of them individually take long. Together they are the moment the truck stops being a stock truck with new wheels and starts being yours.

Wheels and tires, three times over

This is where the weekend got long. Running a 20x9, a 22x9.5, and a 22x12 back to back on one truck means four corners, three times, plus a drive after each one. Running them back to back is also the only honest way to answer the question everybody actually has, which is not can I fit it but which one do I want to live with.

The bottom line

You can build a truck you are proud of in a driveway in one weekend. What you cannot do is start Saturday morning with unopened boxes and no plan.

Yes, and you do not have to be fast to make it. It all comes down to the schedule: Saturday is suspension and lighting, Sunday is intake, exhaust, Flashcal, wheel swaps. The longest job was the first strut side, second side went in half the time. The one thing to hand off is mounting and balancing, since that needs a machine no driveway has.

Offset is the distance between the wheel's mounting surface and the middle of the wheel, measured in millimeters. A high positive number pulls the wheel inward, toward the suspension. A negative number pushes it outward, toward the fender. On this build the 20x9 sits at +1mm, the 22x9.5 at +44mm, and the 22x12 at -51mm, which is why the last one looks so much wider even though the truck never changed.

Backspacing measures the same thing in inches from the back face of the wheel, and most people find it easier to picture: roughly 5in on the 20x9, 7in on the 22x9.5, and 4.5in on the 22x12.
